Fundamentals, Jargon & BuCa 💎

Business Analysis (BA) is not just an administrative documentation step. It is the strategic discipline that converts a business problem or opportunity into a solution that generates tangible for the organization.

Without rigorous analysis, IT projects fail not because of the technology, but because they answer the wrong question. The goal of BA within Information Management is to ensure that we build the right thing before asking how to build it.

The

This is the global bible of corporate strategy best practices. It's not a strict method, but a massive "toolkit" defining how an analyst should extract value and design relevant solutions.

The

The global certification body for Requirements Engineering. It teaches you how to capture, write, model, and trace requirements so an IT developer can understand them flawlessly.

The Genesis: The Business Case (BuCa)

Even before writing the first line of code or drawing a process, you must justify the project's existence and its funding. This is the role of the Business Case (BuCa). It answers 6 existential questions to convince management to invest.

1. WHY ? (Benefits) : Why do this project? We define benefits and the .
2. FOR WHO ? (Users) : Who is impacted? (e.g. The 204 customs team leaders).
3. WHAT ? (Products) : What will we deliver? We use the method to prioritize (prepared by the BA, finally validated by the PO).
4. HOW ? (Costs) : How many Man-Days (Developers, FA, BA) will this cost?
5. HOW LONG ? (Timing) : What is the estimated duration and project phases?
6. WHAT IF ? (Option 0) : What happens if we do NOTHING (Option 0)? And what are the risks?
The BuCa Seasonal Cycle at the FPS:
  • 🌱 Spring: Drafting the preliminary version with experts and the .
  • ☀️ June: The "Pitch"! Oral presentation in front of the committee to defend the project.
  • 🏖️ Summer: Processing feedback and refining the document.
  • 🍂 September: Submitting the final BuCa for budgetary approval for the following year.

Roles: The Great Misunderstanding (BA vs FA) 🎭

The success of a project relies on a strict separation of responsibilities. The myth of the BA as a simple "luxury secretary" or "User Story writer" must disappear. One deals with the "What", the other with the "How".

1. The BA vs FA Demarcation

The Business Analyst (BA)

The guarantor of the "WHAT". The Business Architect.

The BA is the designer of the requirement. They are the official bridge between the customs business and IT. They don't just write down what is dictated to them; they are analysts, facilitators, and translators.

Key responsibilities:
  • Scope Definition (In/Out)
  • Process Modeling ()
  • AS-IS, TO-BE and GAP Analysis
  • Writing Use Cases (Happy & Unhappy flows)
  • Executing/supervising business tests ()

The Functional Analyst (FA)

The guarantor of the "HOW". The Solution Engineer.

The FA takes over once the business requirement is clear and locked in. They translate the "What" (BA's Use Cases) into actionable language for the developers.

Key responsibilities:
  • Technical solution design (Screens, DB)
  • Defining integrations (API between systems)
  • Breaking down into User Stories for coders
  • Writing Acceptance Criteria (AC)
  • 💡 Participates very early in business workshops () to guide towards technically viable solutions.

2. The Executive & Architecture Team

Sponsor & PO

Guarantor of the vision and ROI. The Sponsor funds, the PO validates the MoSCoW and prioritizes the Backlog.

Senior User (Business)

Represents the interests of the final users (e.g., Antwerp customs management).

Senior Supplier (IT)

Represents the IT entity or contractor building and technically delivering the solution.

IT Architect

Defines the overall structure. Gives the initial Go/NoGo, then lets the FA manage the Sprints.

3. The Guardians of Quality and Time

They are the guardians of the temple. We distinguish two vital branches:

  • Quality IT : Checks code cleanliness and architecture.
  • Quality BA : Their role is to verify compliance, not to execute. They don't write tests, they ensure procedures are followed, give formal Business Approval, Go/No-Go advice, and validate Release Notes for external economic operators.

Project Management (PM)

The Project Manager (PM) manages the budget, schedule, and risks. They are supported by the which ensures methodology is respected.

💡 Survival: If you don't have an assigned PM, it's up to you (the BA) to manage the scope and escalate risks!

Strategic Scoping & Fieldwork 🗺️

Before coding anything (Phases 1 to 3), you must map the current state (AS-IS) to project the desired future state (TO-BE). Gap analysis identifies what is missing to move from one to the other.

PESTLE

Examines external factors: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal (very important in Customs!) and Environmental (e.g. CBAM).

MOST

Internal analysis of AGDA: Mission, Objectives, Strategies, and Tactics. Is the project aligned with management's vision?

CATWOE

Identifies: Clients (operators), Actors (customs officers), Transformation, Worldview, Owner (Sponsor), and Environment.

Elicitation (Field data collection)

Elicitation is the art of converting latent needs into explicit requirements. You must not settle for what users say they want, you must understand what they actually need.

The "Silent Master" prompt After an answer, intentionally pause for 3 to 4 seconds. This psychological void pushes the speaker to elaborate and deliver critical details.
Observation (Job Shadowing) Go into the field. Observe what customs officers do. The "workarounds" (post-its stuck on screens) are your best source of real needs.

Expert Advice: Don't isolate the FA!

In the past, the BA did their analysis in their corner, then threw the file "over the wall" to the FA. That's over. Involve the FA from the very first business workshops ()! If they understand the need early, they will prevent you from drawing a TO-BE process that would cost 3 million euros to develop, by immediately proposing viable technical alternatives.

Survival for the Lone BA

In an ideal world, a workshop is led by two people: one BA animating, and a second taking notes. At SSDD, you'll often face your stakeholders alone. How do you survive?

  • Unapologetic recording: On Teams, systematically ask for permission to record. You can then focus 100% on active listening and empathy.
  • Live Scribing (Screen sharing): Project your notes or blank diagram on the screen. Type live with them. If they see an error, they will correct you instantly.
  • The 60-minute rule: Animating and taking notes at the same time is exhausting. Segment your interviews into blocks of one hour maximum to keep a clear mind.

Agile & Prototyping (SpaceX) 🏃‍♂️

The SSDD operates in an Agile framework. To understand it, we must contrast it with the old method: the waterfall model (Waterfall).

The Waterfall Method

We break the project down into sequential phases: 1. Analyze everything, 2. Code everything, 3. Test everything.

  • Tunnel Effect: Customs officers only see the application at the very end of the project (sometimes after 2 years). Often, the final result no longer matches current legislation.

Agile (Scrum)

We break the project down into short cycles (Sprints, from 2 to 4 weeks).

  • Continuous Delivery: At the end of each sprint, we deliver a small piece of testable software that already brings value to the business. If the law changes, we adapt without breaking everything!

Rapid Prototyping (SpaceX Method)

What exactly is prototyping? Instead of writing a 100-page abstract document that no one will read, immediately create a visual aid (a draft BPMN diagram, a fake screen interface, a simplified Excel table). Use this "imperfect" prototype during your next workshop to trigger reactions from the business. How do we avoid "Analysis Paralysis"? By adopting the mindset: "Build, Try, Correct".

  • 1. Present mock-ups, not empty questions: Propose your optimal visual solution based on current info, and let the stakeholders tear it down. Being wrong on a prototype is the fastest way to get the right information!
  • 2. The "Good Enough" diagrams rule: There is no perfect diagram from day one. Don't wait until you have 100% of the info to start drawing. Add detail only iteratively, based on feedback.
  • 3. Words fade away (Retention < 20%): Human memory is not reliable. Decisions made orally over a prototype are quickly forgotten. Always write a decision report. If someone complains, suggest they take responsibility for it at the next meeting.

Artifacts & Writing ✍️

Each step of the cycle generates a specific deliverable (Artifact). They must not be confused. Here is the official dictionary of artifacts produced during the process.

Artifact Author Objective Example & Quality Criteria
Scope Document BA Defines what is included and explicitly excluded from the project (). Ex: "The module will handle H1. Out of Scope: H7."
Criterion: Validated by all.
BPMN (Process) BA Visual mapping of workflows (AS-IS and TO-BE). Ex: Swimlane diagram.
Criterion: Readable end-to-end.
BRD (Business Req.) BA Comprehensive list of business needs decoupled from technology. Ex: "The system must calculate penalties."
Criterion: Traceable and prioritized.
Use Case BA User interaction scenario including Happy & Unhappy Flows. Ex: "UC-01: Submit declaration."
Criterion: Covers errors.
User Story FA Technical unit of work for developers. Ex: "As an Agent, I want..."
Criterion: INVEST format.
Acceptance Criteria FA Exact conditions for a User Story to be validated. Ex: "Given... When... Then..."
Criterion: Testable (Yes/No).

The Art of Precision

The language barrier between "Business" and "IT" causes 90% of bugs. Your writing must be surgical.

To Avoid ❌ To Prefer ✅
"The decision will be sent" (Passive) "The system sends the decision"
"The app must be fast" (Vague) "H1 response time < 2 seconds"

ISO 25010 (Non-Functional Requirements)

An application that follows all business rules can still be a complete failure if it is unusable, unsecure, or crashes every day. This is where ISO 25010 comes in, the international standard for software quality.

Don't just list what the app must do. Specify how it must be: Performance, Reliability (99.9% Uptime), Usability, Security, Maintainability.

Use Case vs User Story

This is the most misunderstood concept. A Use Case is not a big User Story! One is written by the business designer (the BA), the other by the solution engineer (the FA).

The Use Case

By the BA

The overall business behavior.

  • Describes the user's intent to achieve a goal.
  • Completely independent of technology.
  • Essential: The Unhappy Flow. The BA must specify the business rule when things go wrong (e.g., customs error). Analysis is not limited to the ideal scenario!

The User Story

By the FA

The technical implementation broken down.

  • Translates the entire Use Case (both the Happy AND Unhappy Flows) into actionable tasks for coders.
  • Involves technical choices (button, API call).
  • Essential: Acceptance Criteria. Always accompanied by precise ACs to validate technical delivery during the Sprint.

The "Reverse-Review" (BA Sign-off)

Once the FA has written their User Stories and Acceptance Criteria, the process is not over! The BA must review the FA's work. The BA ensures that the technical translation has not distorted the original business need and verifies the "Business Value". The BA then gives formal approval (Sign-off) before the developers start coding the Story.

Practical Case: Antwerp Control

1. The BA (Business) dictates the rule:

"Here is the Unhappy Flow: If the container exceeds 20 tons, IDMS blocks the declaration and requires supervisor approval."

2. The FA (IT) dictates the solution:

"OK. My User Story will create an API call to IAM to verify the supervisor role, and display a red error Pop-up on IDMS."

Golden rule: BAs stop at the rules, FAs don't touch them. The BA doesn't mock it up. The FA doesn't make it up.

Definition of Ready (The Handover)

Passing the baton (Phase 4) is critical. A dossier is only handed over to the technical team if it meets the business analysis Definition of Ready (DoR). If you pass an incomplete analysis to IT, they will code errors.

Handover Checklist (BA to FA)

  • 1. Locked Scope: Validated by the PO. We know what is explicitly "Out of Scope".
  • 2. Validated Processes: BPMN diagrams are approved by field experts.
  • 3. Bulletproof Use Cases: All Unhappy Flows (errors, rejections) are specified.
  • 4. Legal questions settled: Jira tickets to the legal department are resolved and documented with the official answer (`legislative` tag).

Survival & Soft Skills in Meetings

Theory is nice, but the reality for an analyst is dealing with egos and meetings that drag on. The BA is a facilitator and negotiator.

Practical Case: Scope Creep (The fatal button)

The project is validated. Suddenly, a stakeholder says: "By the way, it would be great to have a little 'Export to Excel' button." The developer replies: "That's easy, takes 10 minutes." DANGER! This is Gold Plating.

The BA's role: Kill the idea in the bud.

"This button requires updating the BRD, writing new UAT tests, checking GDPR. Does it meet the objective of the Business Case? No. Out of Scope for V1."

Managing conflicts: Antwerp vs Liège

The Antwerp expert wants a manual process. The Liège expert demands it be automated. What do you do?

  • 1. Stop the emotion: Refocus the debate on the Law. What does the European directive say? (Create a `legislative` ticket).
  • 2. Quantify the disagreement: Document both options and ask the FA to estimate the IT costs for both.
  • 3. Escalate to PO: Present the quantified options to the Product Owner. It is UP TO THEM to make the decision.

Personal Organization

Step 0: The "To-Do" File

Create a SINGLE file (Notepad). Everything that "pops" into your head goes in there. You remove the huge mental load of trying to remember everything.

The Escalation ("Send to Jira")

A stakeholder isn't replying? Send an email with a deadline. To leave an official trace: log into Outlook Web version, use the "Send to Jira" option. You can then create a new ticket or add it to an existing one, including the email. Bulletproof evidence for management!

Ultimate Checklist.

Before declaring victory (DoD):

  • Is the business of the Use Case obvious and aligned with strategy?
  • Have you identified all impacted Stakeholders?
  • Has the Unhappy flow (business error case) been drafted?
  • Does the FA have everything needed to break it down into User Stories?

Excellence in Business Analysis is not innate, it is a method. Respect the BA/FA demarcation, don't be afraid to be wrong fast to correct fast (Prototyping), free your mental load with strict organization, master the art of negotiation (Soft Skills), and use the striking force of the SSDD network.
