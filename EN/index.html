Preface

Why this guide?

The world of customs and excise is often perceived as an administrative fortress, built on complex legal texts, obscure acronyms, and extremely strict IT procedures. Yet, behind this apparent strictness lie fascinating mechanisms that are indispensable to the economic life of our country and the European Union.

This document was born from a simple desire: popularization. It is an attempt to make technical, customs, and tax concepts accessible to everyone. Whether you are a new employee (QA Tester, Business Analyst, Developer) joining the SSDD department, a logistics professional seeking to clarify certain processes, or simply curious about how goods cross our borders, this guide is made for you.

I have chosen to adopt a deliberately direct, conversational, and pedagogical tone, relying on analogies, simplified diagrams, and numerous practical cases. The objective is not to replace legal texts (which remain the only legal reference), but to provide you with the compass and IT map needed to navigate this ecosystem peacefully.

Enjoy the read, and welcome to the border!

Welcome to the Border! 🛂

I will guide you step by step through the mechanisms of customs regulations and accompany you with explanations and questions throughout our journey. Welcome to our guide "Customs and Excise for Dummies".

To start off well: customs is not just a physical barrier at the borders. It is both the security guard and the accountant of an immense shared space: the European Union 🇪🇺. Since the creation of the Customs Union, Member States share a single external border and a common customs tariff. This means that a good from Asia, once cleared in Belgium, can circulate freely to France or Germany without undergoing new customs checks.

What is customs clearance? It is the set of IT and physical formalities to be completed to legally bring a good into or out of the territory. In short: you declare what you transport, its value, its origin, and you pay the corresponding taxes to get the green light (the "release") from customs.

The administration's work is built around three main missions:

  • Taxation: Collecting customs duties (which go to the European budget to protect our economy from foreign competition) and national taxes like VAT.
  • Protection: Checking that imported products (toys, food, medicines) comply with European safety and health standards.
  • Excise: Specific indirect national taxes that hit certain products (alcohol, tobacco, energy products) to regulate their consumption (public health/ecology) and feed state coffers.

Today, this entire ecosystem is fully digitized. Every step involves Belgian IT applications interconnected with the European Commission's databases.

The Customs Identity Card 📜

To understand customs, you first need to visualize its playing field and know who participates in it.

1. The Customs Territory of the Union (CTU) 🌍

The European Union forms a single block against the rest of the world: the Customs Territory of the Union (CTU). Inside this territory, European goods circulate freely from one country to another without customs checks. At the external borders (like the port of Antwerp or Brussels-National airport), customs applies the same rules for all goods entering or leaving the EU.

However, beware of pitfalls! The CTU is not an exact copy of the political or geographical map of Europe. Some political territories of the EU are excluded from customs, and conversely, territories outside the EU are included in it.

🪤 Classic Pitfall Geographical anomalies

Ceuta and Melilla: These two cities are enclaves located on the African continent. Although they are politically Spanish (and therefore in the EU), they are strictly excluded from the Customs Territory of the Union. Sending goods from Madrid to Ceuta therefore obligatorily requires an export declaration! (Note: Even if there are specific agreements allowing 0% customs duties upon arrival, the customs border exists and the computer declaration is mandatory).

Northern Ireland (The post-Brexit exception): Since Brexit, Northern Ireland is politically no longer in the EU. However, to avoid the return of a physical border with Southern Ireland, it continues to apply the Union Customs Code (UCC). It's a virtual border that is extremely complex to manage in IT systems.

Monaco and San Marino: two different exceptions. The Principality of Monaco is a sovereign state outside the EU, but through an agreement with France, it is fully included in the CTU. Sending a parcel from Brussels to Monaco requires no customs declaration.
Conversely, San Marino is bound to the EU by a Customs Union (0% taxes) but is not part of the CTU. Consequence: customs formalities (like presenting a T2 or T2L document) remain mandatory at the border!

Büsingen (DE) and Livigno (IT): The city of Büsingen is German, but it forms an enclave entirely surrounded by Switzerland. Result? It is excluded from the European CTU and attached to the... Swiss customs territory! Consequence: sending a good from Belgium to Büsingen amounts to exporting it to Switzerland. An export customs declaration (AES) is therefore formally mandatory.

2. The EORI Number: The ID Card 🆔

As soon as a company wants to cross this external border to do business, it cannot remain anonymous. Customs requires a unique identification: the EORI number (Economic Operators Registration and Identification). It is used to identify the company in all EU computer applications.

In Belgium, it begins with the country code BE followed by the 10-digit company number. A single number is enough for the entire Union!

How to get an EORI in Belgium? Attention, currently, the procedure is not yet computerized. The request is still mandatorily made by downloading and filling out a specific PDF form (available on the FPS Finance website). A new 100% electronic interface is in development for the future, but for now, the PDF is the only way!

Practical Case: The first contact (ICS2)

A company from Namur buys computers in Tokyo 🇯🇵. These devices are transported by plane to Liege. At what point does the EORI number become indispensable? Well before landing! Thanks to the European security system ICS2, safety data (the ENS) must be sent to customs even before loading the plane in Tokyo. The EORI is already indispensable there to identify the consignee.

Practical Case: The private individual and EORI

A private individual from Namur orders a smartphone in the US for his personal use. Must he request an EORI? No. The EORI is strictly for economic operators (companies). The administration has provided a generic code for personal purchases: BEPRIVATEPERSON.

Practical Case: The foreign company in Antwerp

A company from Paris 🇫🇷 has a French EORI. It imports its goods through the port of Antwerp. Must it request a Belgian EORI? No. The FR number is registered in the central European database (EOS). Belgian customs queries it and validates the identity instantly.

Delegation (Mandates): Europe vs Belgium

Often, the company does not make its declarations itself and delegates this IT task to a broker (customs representative). But beware, access management is divided into two worlds:

  • 🇪🇺
    For European applications (e.g., CDMS, CBAM): Delegation is done via the UUM&DS portal. It is a bilateral agreement: the company grants access, and the broker must electronically accept it on their end for the link to work.
  • 🇧🇪
    For Belgian national applications (e.g., IDMS, AES, AC4): Access does not go through Europe. The company must register an official mandate in favor of its broker via the Belgian authentication system CSAM (the mandate management module linked to MyMinfin).

The Journey of Goods 🚢

Before we go further: what is a classic import? Generally, when a non-EU good arrives, it is declared in the import system IDMS. The company pays customs duties and VAT immediately. Once payment is validated, the good is "released for free circulation": it becomes European, it can circulate and be sold freely anywhere in Europe. But there are logistical exceptions to avoid paying right away...

When a good crosses the border, it must be declared. In Belgium, the administration uses modern IT systems:

Practical Case: The Customs Warehouse (IDMS)

Our company in Namur receives its computers from Tokyo in Liege. Instead of paying customs and VAT immediately, they will store them for 3 months. The solution? The customs warehouse. This regime allows a total suspension of taxes. Payment will only be triggered upon exit (release for free circulation) via the IDMS system.

Practical Case: The Export (AES)

The company in Namur eventually sells half of these stored computers to a customer in Switzerland 🇨🇭 (non-EU). Will they have to pay customs duties? No. The EU does not apply duties upon export. Since they were under tax suspension, they leave without ever having been taxed in Europe. The company uses the AES system to declare the re-export.

Practical Case: Transit (NCTS) and Seals

The Belgian truck crosses France 🇫🇷 to go to Switzerland, filled with these untaxed computers. To secure this journey, the NCTS is used under the external transit regime (T1) with a tracking number (MRN) and a financial guarantee.

At the Swiss border, customs records the arrival and checks the seals. If everything is fine (Satisfactory), the Belgian guarantee is released (Discharge). But what if the seals are broken? Swiss customs reports discrepancies (Transport incident) in NCTS! The system blocks the financial guarantee and triggers an investigation.

Arrival at the Port (IRP and RX/SeaPort)

In large ports like Antwerp-Bruges, managing the massive arrival of containers is a logistical challenge. To streamline these flows, customs uses the IRP (Inbound Release Platform). It is the customs "logistical brain". It communicates continuously with port community platforms (PCS - Port Community Systems) like RX/SeaPort or NxtPort, linking physical terminals, truck drivers, and customs.

Within this port ecosystem, temporary storage management is done via a set of modules:

  • PN (Presentation Notification): This is the alarm signal. The shipping agent notifies the physical arrival of the ship.
  • TSD (Temporary Storage Declaration): The goods are unloaded onto the dock in temporary storage (max 90 days).
  • GA (Goods Accounting): The "general ledger". It keeps track of all stocks present in the port in real time.
  • REN (Re-Export Notification): If the container leaves directly outside the EU, REN is used to evacuate it and "discharge" its stock in GA.

Excise: The Movements 🍷

Why customs duties AND excises?

Customs duties have an economic purpose: they make products manufactured outside the EU more expensive to protect our local companies from foreign competition. Excises, on the other hand, have a public health and ecological purpose. They tax alcohol, tobacco, and fuels to limit their consumption, regardless of whether they are manufactured in Belgium or imported.

And Duty Free?

At airports, "Duty Free" shops are in the international zone. Products (alcohol, perfume) are sold there without customs duties, VAT, and excises, because they are intended to leave the European territory immediately with the passenger. But pay attention to the allowed quantities upon return!

To prevent trucks full of alcohol from simply disappearing into thin air, Europe uses the very strict EMCS (Excise Movement and Control System) to track these products traveling "under duty suspension". The driver must always be accompanied by an electronic document: the e-AD.

The Tax Warehouse

Do not confuse! The customs warehouse manages non-EU goods (customs duties/VAT). The tax warehouse allows you to store goods (EU or not) under suspension of excise duties.

Practical Case: The Wine and the Broken Bottle

A wholesaler in Namur receives a truck of wine from Bordeaux 🇫🇷 covered by an e-AD (via EMCS). He parks it in his tax warehouse. When does he pay the Belgian excise? At the time of release for consumption (the physical exit from the warehouse to the restaurants). To do this, he uses a specific web application called AC4.

What if a pallet falls and bottles break in the warehouse?

Since there was no release for consumption, customs does not demand the payment of excises (provided there is irrefutable proof of destruction for the inspector!).

Special Statuses (CDMS) 🛠️

The customs administration is not just a censor, it is also an economic partner that offers a range of authorizations to facilitate international trade. To ensure harmonization, Europe has centralized these requests.

1. The 22 European Authorizations (CDMS)

Forget local paper forms. Today, there are 22 European customs authorizations that are all centrally managed via a large European portal dedicated to operators: the CDMS (Customs Decisions Management System). Among these 22 authorizations, we find the most strategic ones:

  • CW (Customs Warehousing): The authorization to operate a Customs Warehouse.
  • IP / OP (Inward / Outward Processing): Authorizations for Inward and Outward Processing.
  • CGU (Comprehensive Guarantee): The Comprehensive Guarantee authorization to cover your operations.

2. The Case of Processing (IP / OP)

These "tailor-made" regimes, whose authorization (IP/OP) is managed in the CDMS, allow goods to be processed internationally without paying the full taxes.

A. Inward Processing (IP)

Importing engines from China, assembling them into cars in Belgium, then exporting the finished cars to Morocco. The factory uses code H4 in IDMS to obtain a total suspension of taxes upon import.

B. Outward Processing (OP)

A workshop in Tournai exports Belgian fabric to Tunisia to sew it, then re-imports the finished suits. Upon return, it will only pay taxes on the foreign added value (the labor).

🪤 Classic Pitfall The missing authorization

The car factory tries to import its Chinese engines. The forwarder sends the declaration (code H4 for Inward Processing) in IDMS. But beware: if the factory has not made its request in the European system and the authorization (IP) is not validated and active in the CDMS, the Belgian IT system IDMS immediately rejects the declaration. The company will have to clear normally (H1) by paying the full rate.

3. The Labyrinth of Guarantees (GUM, FINDA)

To benefit from inward processing, the State requires a financial guarantee to cover the amount of suspended taxes. Do not get your wires crossed between the different systems:

🇪🇺
GUM (Guarantee Management): The centralized European system. Linked to CDMS, it manages the global authorization (CGU) and monitors its use across several Member States.
🇧🇪
FINDA: The purely Belgian accounting software. It concretely applies the blocking of your funds during a national import/export declaration (IDMS/AES).

Practical Case: The revolving account in FINDA

The Charleroi factory has a guarantee of 100,000 €. They already have 90,000 € of engines in the factory. A new container arrives with 20,000 € of potential taxes. What does FINDA do? It blocks the whole container! IDMS does not split the declaration.

The logistical or financial solution: Either the factory finally exports the finished cars outside the EU via AES (FINDA then re-credits the 90,000 € to the balance, this is the revolving account), or it simply pays the taxes due on the old engines to immediately release guarantee for the new container.

4. The trust label: AEO

This is the highest certification in the customs world (Authorized Economic Operator). It comes in different types according to your needs (AEOC, AEOS, AEOF). This status is issued by Europe and is only valid within the European Union.

The role of MRAs (Mutual Recognition Agreement)

Thanks to mutual recognition agreements (MRA) signed between the EU and third countries (like the United States or Japan), European customs recognizes this foreign label as the equivalent of the AEO. The level of trust increases and the risk decreases drastically, facilitating international flows.

5. Postponed accounting for VAT: ET14000

The ET14000 authorization is a purely fiscal mechanism linked to Belgian VAT, managed via MyMinfin. It allows avoiding paying VAT at the customs office during import. The tax is directly postponed (reverse-charged) onto the company's periodic VAT return, canceling the immediate financial impact on cash flow.

The Language of Datasets 💻

Forget the old paper Single Administrative Document (SAD). Today, with the Union Customs Code (UCC), Europe has harmonized the structure of information via Datasets.

Imagine these datasets as boxes of LEGOs. The administration does not ask you for the same box depending on what you want to build. Europe has classified these codes by "families" represented by a letter.

1. The "H" family: Import (IDMS) 📥

  • H1 Standard Import: The full set for "release for free circulation".
  • H2 Customs warehouse: Placing in warehouse without paying taxes.
  • H3 Temporary admission: Ex: Bringing in an artwork for an exhibition.
  • H4 Inward processing: Importing parts under suspension (IP).

The H7 code for E-commerce 🛍️

The H7 code is a revolution. Before it, every imported parcel required a full H1, unmanageable for platforms handling thousands of shipments.

The H7 is designed specifically for shipments whose value does not exceed 150 €.

  • Simplification: Lightened dataset for massive and fast processing.
  • IOSS Link (Import One-Stop Shop): Allows declaring the VAT already collected during the sale on the Asian website, avoiding reclaiming it from the individual upon arrival.

Practical Case: The H7 fraud risk

What is the risk if a company systematically declares its parcels as "H7" even when their value exceeds 150 €? The risk is a colossal dead loss of customs duties and VAT. By falsely declaring an 800 € smartphone under the H7 code (falsified value at 140 €), the company evades the strict controls of the H1 and normal duties. This is a fraudulent undervaluation that customs massively track down via their risk algorithms.

UPDATE ALERT: The Small Parcels Revolution (July 2026) 🚨

Until recently, e-commerce enjoyed a golden advantage: any parcel valued under €150 (the famous H7) entered Europe without paying a single cent of customs duties. Note, there is a common confusion: VAT IS due from the very first cent! The €22 VAT exemption was abolished in 2021.

Since July 1, 2026, the €150 customs exemption is also abolished. From now on, a flat-rate and temporary customs duty of €3 per item (per declaration line) applies to all distance sales under €150 (B2C).

The H7 Thunderbolt

This flat-rate duty (tax code A00) applies whether the IOSS system is used or not. The goal? To protect our local businesses from unfair competition and to curb the ecological impact of massive shipments of cheap products.

Practical Case: Online shopping, 2026 version

You order on a famous Asian platform: 1 phone case (€5) and 1 USB cable (€3). Your basket is worth €8. The parcel is under €150, so it will be declared under the H7 format.

  • Before July 1, 2026: You paid €8 + about €1.68 VAT. Zero customs duties. The deal of the century.
  • Today: Customs will apply the flat-rate duty of €3 per item line. Since the case and the cable are different products (different customs codes), there are two lines. You will therefore pay €6 in customs duties (€3 x 2 items), plus the VAT calculated on this new total! Your €3 cable has just become much more expensive.

The "Dummies" Exception (C2C): Note that if your aunt in Canada sends you a hand-knitted sweater worth €40 (Gift from private individual to private individual - C2C), this €3 tax does not apply! It is only aimed at B2C trade flows.

Centralised Clearance (CCE) 🌍

We have seen the datasets (H1, etc.). But where should this data be sent? Historically, the goods had to be cleared where they physically arrived. Europe has taken a major step by separating the physical flow from the administrative flow with CCE (Centralised Clearance at Import/Export).

Thanks to CCE, a company can lodge its import declaration in the system of its own country (where it is established), even if the goods physically arrive in another Member State. The two customs administrations communicate with each other behind the scenes in an automated way.

Attention to VAT: Even if the H1 declaration is processed and customs duties are paid in Belgium, national VAT remains due in the country of final destination of the goods! This significantly complicates accounting.

Practical Case: The container in Rotterdam

A company from Namur imports toys from China. The ship physically arrives at the port of Rotterdam (Netherlands). Instead of mandating a Dutch broker and paying taxes in the Netherlands, the company uses CCE. It lodges its H1 dataset into IDMS (the Belgian system) from its headquarters in Namur, and pays its taxes in Belgium. The Belgian computer contacts the Dutch computer to give the order to release the container in Rotterdam. It's that simple!

Returned Goods Relief 🔄

We have seen how to import and export. But what happens if the exported goods turn around and come back to you? This is a very frequent logistical case, particularly in e-commerce: the regime of Returned Goods Relief.

The basic principle of customs is to tax import. But if you re-import your *own* product (which had been manufactured or already cleared in the EU) within 3 years and in the same state, you are not going to pay customs duties again. Customs grants a total "relief", provided you can prove that the object is indeed the same.

Practical Case: The scratched watch

A Belgian watchmaker exports a watch to a customer in Switzerland (non-EU) via an AES declaration (B1). The Swiss customer receives the watch, notices it is scratched, and sends it back immediately to Belgium. Upon return of the package, the Belgian declarant uses the IDMS system. By entering the correct relief code (F03) and providing the MRN of the initial export declaration to prove the identity of the watch, he re-imports it paying €0 in duties and €0 in VAT!

Transit (NCTS) and its Vault (GMS) 🔄

We have seen that when goods travel across Europe without paying taxes, they do so under the transit regime via the NCTS system. Because the State is taking a risk, a financial guarantee is required via a virtual "vault": the GMS (Guarantee Management System).

1. T1 or T2: What is the fundamental difference?

Before sending a truck on the road, you must choose the right transit regime. Don't mix them up!

  • External Transit (T1): Concerns non-European goods (which have not yet paid their taxes). T1 allows these goods to be moved from point A to point B in the EU while suspending duties and taxes.
  • Internal Transit (T2): Concerns European goods. Why do a transit if they are already European? Because the goods will cross a third country (e.g., a truck going from Italy to Germany via Switzerland). The T2 proves to German customs upon return that the goods were already European at departure!

2. Arrival and Control (NCTS Phases 5 & 6)

What happens when the truck arrives at its destination? Modern NCTS has become extremely strict.

The Waiting Timer

As soon as the arrival is accepted, a countdown starts (e.g., 15 min). This gives customs time to decide whether to perform a physical control.

Unloading Remarks (Satisfactory)

If everything is perfect, the declaration is notified as "Conform" (Satisfactory). The guarantee in the GMS is released.

The Discrepancies

If the seal is broken or a package is missing, "Not satisfactory" is reported. The NCTS then demands to know the holder's reaction: No opposition (accepts to pay the debt on the blocked guarantee), Opposition (disputes the fine), or Undecided (the guarantee remains blocked pending).

Practical Case: The broken seal and the blocked guarantee

A truck arrives in Antwerp. The agent notices that the seal number does not match the one at departure. He enters "State of seals : Not OK" in the *Unloading remarks*. What does NCTS do? It immediately blocks the release of the guarantee in the GMS! The administration will claim the potential duties, as an unauthorized manipulation may have occurred en route.

The equation with three unknowns: Tariff, Origin, Value.

The Passport

The Tax Base (€)

The Tariff Classification and TARBEL

Customs does not read descriptions, it reads numbers: the first 6 digits are the Harmonized System (HS) (worldwide), completed by the Combined Nomenclature and the TARIC (Europe, 10 digits).

The indispensable tool: TARBEL.
To find this code, declarants use the TARBEL application (the Belgian Tariff). It combines the TARIC database (duties, anti-dumping) with Belgian measures (VAT, Excises). It's the classifier's bible.

🪤 Classic Pitfall The DNA error

A company imports high-end touch tablets (subject to taxes), but the declarant makes a mistake in TARBEL and uses the HS code for "digital photo frames" (taxed at 0%). During a check, the customs officer turns on the screen: tax reassessment and fine for false declaration!

Origin and Value

Origin: Do not confuse provenance and origin. Preferential origin (free trade agreements) can reduce duties to 0%. To prove this, an EUR.1 certificate (the official "passport" validating the origin of goods) is used.

Value: Customs taxes the "transaction value" up to the European border (including product cost + sea freight + insurance). Watch out for Royalties: If you import blank t-shirts for €2 but pay a €5 license to print a major brand logo on them, this €5 must mandatorily be added to the customs value!

🪤 Classic Pitfall The fake Swiss passport

You buy bikes in Geneva (Swiss provenance). But the motor and frame are made in Taiwan. The Swiss company just stuck a label on it. The real origin is Taiwanese: the EUR.1 certificate is invalid, and you will pay full duties.

🪤 Classic Pitfall The expensive oversight

A company declares a container but "forgets" to include the €3,000 of sea transport in the Declared Value (H1). During an audit, it will have to reimburse the difference in VAT and customs on this €3,000.

Mini-Quiz: Test your knowledge

A container leaves the United States. The value of the goods is €10,000. Sea transport costs €2,000. On what basis (tax base) will Belgian customs calculate the import duties?

Answer: On €12,000. Customs taxes the value "up to the EU border", so transport and insurance costs must mandatorily be included in the calculation!

The Security Shield (ICS2) 🛡️

Today, with ICS2 (Import Control System 2), the rule is simple: no data, no load. The carrier must send a security mini-declaration (the ENS) to Europe even before the goods are in the plane at the other side of the world.

Practical Case: The "bomb" in the box

An individual tries to ship a suspicious package with explosives from the Middle East to Brussels via a cargo plane. Thanks to the PLACI module of ICS2, Belgian customs receives the data before loading. The algorithm detects a critical risk (Assessment "Do Not Load"). The package will never board the plane at the other side of the world.

Sanctions & Dual-Use 🚫

Customs is not just a tax collector, it is also the geopolitical armed branch of the European Union. In times of conflict or international crisis, free trade stops where restrictions begin. Importing or exporting is no longer an absolute right.

1. Sanctions and Embargoes

Economic sanctions aim to put pressure on a country, a government, or even specific individuals (oligarchs, warlords). They take two main forms in the customs software:

  • Trade Embargo (Goods) Strict prohibition to export certain products to a targeted country (e.g., prohibition to sell European luxury products or high-tech) or to import from it (e.g., prohibition to buy Russian oil or diamonds to cut off their funding).
  • Blacklists (Asset freezing) Even if the merchandise itself is not dangerous, it is formally forbidden to do business with people or companies placed on a European "Blacklist". Customs freezes their goods at the port.

Practical Case: The blocked luxury watches

An Antwerp jeweler tries to export €200,000 worth of luxury watches to a country under European sanctions. The customs AES system analyzes the product's DNA (the watch's HS code) and the destination country. A red alert sounds: so-called "luxury" products are subject to a strict embargo to this destination. The declaration is refused, the export is cancelled, the merchandise is seized, and the jeweler faces criminal prosecution.

2. Dual-Use Goods

Particular attention is paid to Dual-Use Goods. These are goods created for strictly civil use, but whose technology, materials, or software could be diverted to manufacture weapons or military equipment (chemical, nuclear, etc.). Exporting these goods is not subject to a total embargo, but requires very strict export licenses.

Practical Case: The militarized washing machine

A Belgian company produces high-quality electronic chips for industrial washing machines (a 100% civil good). It receives a large order from a country currently under geopolitical tension. During the export declaration (AES), the customs algorithm (SEDA) blocks the operation. Why? Because intelligence services have discovered that the army of this country recovers these civil chips to equip the guidance systems of its combat drones. The "Dual-Use" license will be categorically refused.

13

Green Customs 🌿

In 2026, the customs officer is the first line of defense of European ecology. The EU wants to be climate-neutral and uses customs to ensure that the rest of the world respects our efforts.

CBAM (Carbon Tax)

Importing steel from a country that does not tax CO2 will oblige the company to buy "carbon certificates" from customs (to compensate for the foreign factory's pollution and balance prices).

EUDR (Deforestation)

To import cocoa or wood, the declarant must prove (via strict GPS coordinates) that the plot where the product grew was not recently deforested. Otherwise? Immediate blockade in Antwerp.

14

The Single Window 🤝

Customs protects health, environment, and consumers, but it does not work alone! In the field, other government agencies (like the FASFC in Belgium for food safety) are responsible for sanitary or veterinary controls.

To avoid an operator having to present paper certificates to three different agencies, Europe has set up the Single Window (EU CSW-CERTEX). It is a gigantic IT highway where the customs application (IDMS) automatically communicates with the applications of other agencies (like TRACES for the FASFC).

Practical Case: The container of pineapples

A Belgian importer brings a container of fresh pineapples from Costa Rica. His declarant submits the H1 declaration via IDMS. Before giving its approval, the application automatically queries the Single Window system to know if the FASFC has validated the phytosanitary certificate. If the FASFC inspector has not checked the "OK" box in his own software (TRACES), customs blocks the H1 declaration within a second. The two administrations work in perfect synchronization without making a single phone call.

15

The Customs Radar (SEDA 3.0) 🎯

How does customs choose which container to open out of the millions that pass through each year? The administration does not choose randomly, it relies on highly advanced data analysis: this is the role of the rule engine SEDA 3.0.

1. European communication: RIF and CRMS

Risk does not stop at borders. If French customs discovers a new method of concealing cocaine, it writes a file, a RIF (Risk Information Form). This is a security alert sent to all Member States via the CRMS (Customs Risk Management System). The Belgian algorithm (SEDA) then immediately integrates this threat into its calculations.

2. Artificial Intelligence: SEDA 3.0

Before, the system was rigid: either the declaration was given the green light, or it was blocked on red. Today, the SEDA 3.0 engine calculates a continuous Risk Score (between 0 and 1) for each declaration. The closer to 1, the more critical it is.

This score is calculated according to 4 main mathematical pillars:

1. Risk (Probability): Based on the operator's history and suspicious routes.
2. Impact: Is it financially important? It calculates the potentially lost money (taxes) or the physical weight.
3. Importance: A multiplier. If the operator is on a Blacklist, the score explodes!
4. Random: The element of surprise. The system sometimes selects perfect declarations to test the models and cover tracks.

Practical Case: The Cancelled AEO Bonus

Remember the AEO status (Chapter 4). In SEDA, the AEO applies a "Facilitation": the algorithm mathematically reduces your probability of being controlled (e.g., -50% score). But beware: If SEDA detects an absolute Embargo alert (international sanctions) or if the name of one of the actors is on a Blacklist (proven fraud), the algorithm literally "cuts" this advantage. The AEO bonus is cancelled and the score goes back up to 1 (Mandatory priority control).

3. The Filter (Cutoff) and the MRA Interface

Thousands of declarations get a score. But customs does not have the staff to check 100% of containers. SEDA therefore uses a Cutoff threshold. If the threshold is set to 0.70, all declarations below it are ignored by the computer.

Those that exceed the threshold end up on the customs officers' screen (the CRC field team). This decision screen is called the MRA (Manual Risk Analysis) tool.

BEWARE OF THE JARGON: The Pitfall of the two "MRAs"!

The customs world loves acronyms, so much so that they use the same acronym for two completely different things!

  • In Chapter 4 (Special Statuses), MRA means Mutual Recognition Agreement (the international agreement that recognizes the AEO status abroad).
  • Here (Risk Analysis), MRA means Manual Risk Analysis (the software where the customs officer decides whether to control a suspicious declaration). Do not mix them up!

4. The Action: The Three Control Levels

Once the declaration is targeted on the MRA screen, the customs officer takes action. He has three levels of intervention:

Level 1: Documentary Control

The customs officer remotely checks the consistency of the Golden Triptych by requesting the paperwork (invoices, EUR.1 certificates) via the system.

Level 2: Scanner Control (X-ray)

The container passes through an X-ray scanner at the terminal. The customs officer analyzes the radiographic image to spot suspicious densities without opening the doors.

Level 3: Physical Control (Inspection)

The ultimate level. The container is opened and physically inspected (sometimes with sniffer dogs or specialized teams) to ensure that reality matches the declaration.
